Before we start

Design confirmed and reviewed. Equipment and materials ordered and checked in. Any subcontracted work — electrical work, for example — arranged with appropriately qualified contractors. Access, parking, protection, pipe routes and your expectations all agreed in advance, along with a risk assessment for the work.

This is the part customers never see and the part that decides whether the week goes well. Almost every installation that goes badly goes badly for a reason that was knowable beforehand: a cylinder that will not fit up the stairs, a pipe route nobody had looked at, an electrical supply that turns out to need the network operator, or a delivery with nowhere to park.

So there is a pre-start check, and it is a real one. Nothing mobilises until it is done.

The pre-start check

  • Design and equipment confirmed against the proposal
  • Deliveries booked and checked in
  • Subcontractors briefed on the programme and the customer standard
  • Access, parking and welfare agreed
  • Protection and pipe routes agreed
  • Your expectations, including the period without heating
  • Risk assessment for the work

Who this is for

  • Homeowners who have had a survey and accepted a proposal
  • Renovations where heating is one part of a larger programme
  • Properties needing emitter or hot-water work alongside the heat pump

What actually gets installed

A heat pump installation is not one appliance. It is an outdoor unit, indoor plant, a cylinder, emitter changes, controls and the pipework that ties them together — installed to a design that was reviewed before anyone ordered anything.

Outdoor unit and pipework

Mounted level and clear of the ground in the agreed position, with insulated flow and return pipework, a condensate route that drains safely, and electrical isolation where it can be reached.

Cylinder and hot water

Cylinder installed in the agreed position with the controls and safety arrangements its type requires. An unvented cylinder brings additional building regulation requirements, and the documentation for our scope comes with it.

Hot water and cylinders explained

Emitter changes

The radiators the design identified, and only those. Some rooms keep what they have. Where a bathroom towel rail cannot deliver enough heat at the design temperature, a larger rail or an extra emitter is designed in rather than discovered later.

Will I need new radiators?

Controls

Weather compensation configured from the heat loss calculation, with an outside sensor, and set up for steady low-temperature running rather than left on factory defaults.

Hydraulics and electrical

Pipe sizes and flow rates to the design, the system flushed, filled and balanced, and electrical work carried out by appropriately qualified contractors working to Airwarm’s design and coordination.

Commissioning and records

Every test recorded, concealed pipework photographed before it is covered, components labelled and any change from the design written down with its reason.

Standards on site

Your house is not a building site, and it is where somebody lives. These are written standards rather than good intentions.

Your home is protected

Floors and furniture covered, materials stored tidily, dust and noise controlled as far as the work allows, and the property left safe every evening.

You know what is happening

A daily update on what was done and what happens next. If something changes, you hear it from us on the day rather than finding out later.

The work is recorded

Concealed pipework photographed before it is covered, components labelled, and any change from the design written down with the reason.

Commissioning is a stage, not a formality

Flushing, filling, pressure testing, electrical confirmation, control setup, weather compensation, balancing, hot-water checks and a noise check. A system is not finished simply because it is producing heat.

Subcontractors work to the same standard

Written scope, agreed price, the same conduct in your home, the same evidence requirements. Subcontracting does not move Airwarm’s responsibility to you onto somebody else.

Siting the outdoor unit

The position is decided at survey, not on the first morning. By the time the unit arrives, the mounting, the clearances, the condensate route and the noise assessment have all been settled, and you have agreed to what it will look like.

That matters most on the sort of streets Airwarm works on. A yard shared with a neighbour, a ginnel, a narrow side passage or a single external elevation all constrain the answer, and the trade-off between the best technical position and the least obtrusive one is a conversation to have with you rather than a decision to make for you.

Where a property is listed or in a conservation area, consent comes before design. Airwarm establishes that early and tells you what would be required.

Commissioning, in full

This is the difference between a system that produces heat and a system that performs. Every step is carried out and recorded.

  1. Flushing and filling

    The system cleaned and filled properly, because debris from old pipework is what wrecks new plant.

  2. Pressure testing

    Tested before anything is boxed in or covered.

  3. Electrical confirmation

    Supply, isolation and protection confirmed and documented by the contractor who carried it out.

  4. Control setup

    Configured for this house, this design and this household.

  1. Weather compensation

    The compensation curve set from the heat loss calculation rather than a default.

  2. Balancing

    Flow adjusted circuit by circuit so every room gets what the design intended.

  3. Hot-water checks

    Recovery, temperatures and the safety arrangements the cylinder type requires.

  4. Noise check and handover

    Sound checked in the position it was assessed for, then the documentation and the handover conversation.

Handover, and what happens after

You get a plain-English guide to your system: what the settings do, what normal behaviour looks like through the seasons, what to check before calling anyone, warranty information and the service schedule.

Then we stay in contact: a follow-up within 48 hours, a comfort review at two weeks once you have lived with the system, seasonal optimisation where it is needed and an annual service reminder. The two-week review is the useful one, because it is when the controls are refined against how you actually use the house rather than how the design assumed you would.

Airwarm does not offer a 24-hour or emergency call-out service. We will tell you what our actual response looks like rather than implying something we cannot deliver. If you need cover of that kind, it is better to know now.

And if something goes wrong, you contact us. Complaints are acknowledged promptly, the facts established, a comfort expectation distinguished from a technical fault, ownership assigned, the next action agreed with you and the outcome documented. Defensive communication is not acceptable to us.

Frequently asked questions

How long does an installation take?

It depends on how much emitter and pipework change is needed, whether a cylinder is being installed where there was none, and what electrical work is required. Airwarm gives you a realistic programme in the proposal rather than a standard figure, and confirms it at the pre-start stage.

Will I be without heating or hot water?

There is normally a period without heating and hot water while the changeover happens. Airwarm plans that period deliberately, tells you how long it is expected to be, and agrees it with you before work starts rather than presenting it as an unavoidable surprise.

How much mess and damage should I expect?

Some lifting of floorboards or carpet edges and some making good is usual where pipework has to be rerouted. The survey identifies the routes in advance so that what is needed is known rather than discovered. Airwarm’s expectation of its own installations is respectful workmanship and a tidy site.

Will it be noisy for my neighbours?

The noise level at the nearest neighbouring window is assessed as part of the design, and it is a condition of the permitted development route. Position and mounting matter more than the equipment. Airwarm would rather discuss a position that works for the neighbours at survey stage than deal with a complaint afterwards.

Who does the electrical work?

A heat pump is a substantial electrical load, so the incoming supply, main fuse and consumer unit are all checked at survey. Electrical work is carried out by appropriately qualified regional electrical contractors working to Airwarm’s design and coordination, and where the network operator needs to be involved Airwarm will say so early.

How much does a heat pump installation cost?

Airwarm is not publishing prices ahead of its trading launch, and a figure quoted without a survey would be a guess. What determines the cost is the size of the system, how many emitters need changing, the cylinder and its position, the pipework and electrical work required, and access. The proposal sets all of it out itemised.

What warranty will I have?

There is the manufacturer’s product warranty, whose length and conditions depend on the equipment, and Airwarm’s own responsibility for its workmanship. The warranty process is being finalised ahead of launch and the terms will be set out in the proposal rather than described vaguely.

What documents will I receive?

A documented design showing the heat loss calculation and its assumptions, the emitter schedule, the equipment specification, a commissioning record, manufacturer documentation and warranty details, and a handover pack explaining how the system is meant to be operated.

What building regulations apply?

Heating work is subject to building regulations, and an unvented hot water cylinder brings additional requirements. Your building control body confirms what applies to your project overall, and Airwarm provides the documentation for its own scope.

What happens after installation?

Airwarm contacts you within 48 hours, carries out a comfort review at two weeks, provides seasonal optimisation where required, and sends an annual service reminder. The two-week review is when the controls are refined against how you actually live with the system.

What if something goes wrong afterwards?

You contact us. Complaints are acknowledged promptly, the facts established, ownership assigned, the next action agreed with you and the outcome documented. Defensive communication is not acceptable to us. There is no 24-hour or emergency call-out service, and we will not imply otherwise.
