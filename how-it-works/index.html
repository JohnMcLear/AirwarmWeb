The eight stages

  1. Quick Home Energy Assessment

    Four short stages online, a few minutes, no photographs and nothing to measure. You get an indicative view of where your property stands straight away.

    Your answers are worked out in your own browser. Nothing is sent to Airwarm unless you choose to send it — either with the short form at the end or by e-mailing us — which means you decide what to share and when.

    Your decision: whether to send us a note about your result. start your Airwarm assessment.

  2. Airwarm reviews the information

    A person reads your answers. If something looks like it needs clarifying, or looks like a genuine obstacle, we would rather find it now than three visits later.

    Common things we look for at this point: a property type where siting the outdoor unit is likely to be the deciding question, a combination boiler with no obvious cylinder position, a listed or leasehold property where permission comes before design, and an address at the edge of the area we can properly cover.

    Your decision: nothing yet. Ours is whether a call is worth your time.

  3. You decide

    You get our review in writing: what we found, what we assumed, what would need to change and whether a full survey is worth anyone’s time. If it is not, we say so and explain why. Then it is your decision, in your own time. There is no call to sit through and nothing to sign.

    This is also where we are honest about sequence. Sometimes the sensible order is insulation or emitter work first and a heat pump afterwards, and that is a useful answer even though it is not the quick one.

    Your decision: whether to book a survey.

  4. Detailed home survey, where it is justified

    Room dimensions, construction, insulation, windows, existing emitters, pipe routes, cylinder options, outdoor-unit positions, condensate, electrical supply and access. This is an engineering visit, not a sales appointment.

    Digital measuring tools, voice notes and photographs are used as evidence aids, alongside a structured manual verification process rather than instead of it. Where practical, the people who would install the system take part in or review the survey.

    Your decision: none on the day. You are not asked to sign anything at the kitchen table. What the survey covers.

  5. Design and a clear proposal

    Heat loss, emitter sizing, flow temperature, hydraulics, hot water, controls and siting are designed and reviewed before anything is issued. You get the assumptions as well as the answers, in writing.

    The proposal sets out the property summary, what you said you wanted, the design assumptions, the recommended equipment and its output, the emitter schedule, the cylinder, the controls, the electrical scope, what is excluded, the expected programme, warranty, aftercare and the payment schedule. It also says what could change it and why.

    Your decision: whether to proceed, ask for a variation, or stop. All three are reasonable.

  6. Planned installation

    Design confirmed, equipment ordered and checked in, any subcontracted work arranged, and access, parking, protection, pipe routes, welfare and your expectations agreed before anyone arrives.

    On site: floors and furniture protected, tidy storage, dust and noise controlled, concealed pipework photographed before it is covered, components labelled, changes recorded with the reason, a daily update, and the property left safe each evening.

    Your decision: the dates, and anything you want handled differently in your home. How installation works.

  7. Commissioning, handover and optimisation

    Flushing, filling, pressure testing, electrical confirmation, control setup, weather compensation, balancing, hot-water checks and a noise check — then documentation and a proper handover. A system is not finished simply because it is producing heat.

    Handover is a conversation, not a folder. What the settings do, what normal behaviour looks like through the seasons, what to check before calling anyone, and what not to fiddle with. The single most useful thing to understand is weather compensation, because it is what lets the system run at the lowest useful temperature.

    Your decision: whether you genuinely understand the system. If not, we go round again.

  8. Ongoing servicing and support

    Follow-up contact shortly after handover, a comfort review once you have lived with it, seasonal optimisation where it is needed and an annual service reminder.

    The two-week review is the useful one. It is when the controls are refined against how you actually live with the system rather than how the design assumed you would.

    Your decision: whether to keep the servicing with us. We would like you to, and we will not make it awkward if you would rather not.

What Airwarm will not do

Quote without seeing the house

A number produced from a postcode and a bedroom count is a guess. It is also the easiest thing in the world to produce, which is why so many people are given one.

Tell you every home is suitable

Some are not, or not yet. You will get the reason and the practical route forward, not a brush-off and not a survey fee spent finding out something we could have told you.

Leave you to work out the controls

Handover includes what the settings do and what normal behaviour looks like. A system nobody understands gets turned up, and then it costs more to run than it should.

Promise a savings figure

Running cost depends on your building, your tariff, the efficiency achieved and how you use the system. We will explain all four rather than reduce them to a number that flatters us.

Pressure you at the kitchen table

No discount that expires this evening, no signature at the end of a survey visit. Proposals are read at leisure, and questions are expected.

Imply cover we do not offer

There is no 24-hour or emergency call-out service. Response expectations are stated plainly, so that if you need cover of that kind you know now.

What you end up holding

A heating system is only as good as the record of how it was designed and set up. Undocumented work is a problem that gets inherited — by you, by the next engineer, and by whoever buys the house.

So the paperwork is part of the job rather than an afterthought, and it is written to be read by the person who lives there.

After the installation

Four checkpoints, in the first year and then annually. This is the schedule Airwarm is committing to.

  1. Within 48 hours

    A call to check the system is behaving as expected and that nothing about it is puzzling you.

  2. At two weeks

    A comfort review. This is where the controls are refined against how you actually use the house, which is nearly always slightly different from the design assumption.

  3. Seasonally, where required

    Optimisation as the weather changes, particularly through the first winter. Weather compensation usually benefits from one round of refinement in real cold.

  4. Annually

    A service reminder. There is no gas safety check on a heat pump, but system water, filters and strainers, pressures, controls, weather compensation, hot water operation, fault history, emitters and noise all need checking — and manufacturers commonly require documented servicing for the warranty to remain valid. What an annual service covers.

If something goes wrong

Airwarm acknowledges promptly, establishes the facts, distinguishes a comfort expectation from a technical fault, assigns ownership, agrees the next action and documents the outcome. Defensive communication is not acceptable. A published complaints procedure will set out the route including escalation.

Questions about the process

What if the assessment says my home is not currently suitable?

You will be told which constraint caused that and what would resolve it. It is a statement about the property’s condition today, not a permanent verdict, and there is nearly always something worth doing even if a heat pump is not the immediate next step.

Do I have to have a survey to get advice?

No. The assessment and our written review come first, and both are there to work out whether a survey makes sense at all.

Is a survey chargeable?

Airwarm’s commercial terms are being finalised ahead of its trading launch, and the survey arrangement will be confirmed clearly before you commit to anything. It will never be a surprise on an invoice.

How long does an installation take?

It depends on how much emitter and pipework change is needed, whether a cylinder is being installed where there was none, and what electrical work is required. Airwarm gives you a realistic programme in the proposal rather than a standard figure, and confirms it at the pre-start stage.

Will I be without heating or hot water?

There is normally a period without heating and hot water while the changeover happens. Airwarm plans that period deliberately, tells you how long it is expected to be, and agrees it with you before work starts rather than presenting it as an unavoidable surprise.

What happens after installation?

Airwarm contacts you within 48 hours, carries out a comfort review at two weeks, provides seasonal optimisation where required, and sends an annual service reminder. The two-week review is when the controls are refined against how you actually live with the system.

What documents will I receive?

A documented design showing the heat loss calculation and its assumptions, the emitter schedule, the equipment specification, a commissioning record, manufacturer documentation and warranty details, and a handover pack explaining how the system is meant to be operated.

What if the survey says my house needs work first?

Then that is what we tell you, along with what specifically would need to change and roughly in what order. Plenty of properties are a good candidate after some fabric or emitter work, and knowing that is a useful answer in itself.
