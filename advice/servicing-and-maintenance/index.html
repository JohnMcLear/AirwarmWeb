Heat pump servicing and maintenance: what it involves

An annual service checks that a heat pump is still running as it was designed to, not just that it is still running. It covers the system water, filters and strainers, pressures, controls, weather compensation, hot water operation, the fault history and the outdoor unit, and it is usually a condition of keeping a manufacturer’s warranty valid.

What it actually means

A heat pump has fewer things to go wrong than a gas boiler in one respect: there is no combustion, no flue and no gas, so there is no annual gas safety check.

What it does have is a sealed refrigerant circuit, a water circuit through your radiators or underfloor pipes, a hot water cylinder, an electrical supply, a fan and a set of controls configured to a specific design. Servicing is mostly about the water side, the electrical side and the settings, rather than the refrigerant.

The refrigerant circuit in a domestic heat pump is normally factory-sealed and is not routinely topped up. If a system needs refrigerant, that indicates a leak, and the correct response is to find and repair it rather than simply recharge. Any work on the refrigerant circuit must be carried out by someone with the appropriate refrigerant handling qualification.

The other distinctive part is the review of settings and performance. A boiler service was largely a safety and condition check. A heat pump service should also ask whether the system is still doing what it was designed to do, because a system quietly running hotter than intended will keep the house warm while using more electricity than it should.

Why it matters for the outcome in your home

Performance drifts. Water quality degrades, filters and strainers collect debris, pressures fall slowly, and settings get changed. None of these announce themselves, and a system can lose efficiency gradually without ever showing a fault code.

Small problems become large ones. A partially blocked strainer restricts flow, which makes the heat pump work harder and can eventually trigger protection cutouts or damage components. Catching it at a service is straightforward; discovering it in January is not.

Warranties usually depend on it. Manufacturers commonly require documented annual servicing for the warranty to remain valid, and the requirement varies by product. This should be stated clearly in your handover documentation rather than buried.

Water quality protects the equipment. Correct inhibitor levels, filtration and, where relevant, glycol concentration all need checking rather than assuming.

What you can do between services

Not much needs doing, which is the point, but four things genuinely help.

  • Keep the outdoor unit clear. It needs air flow, so leaves, snow drifts, shrubs, bins and garden furniture all matter. Do not enclose it in a decorative box that restricts air flow.
  • Leave the settings alone unless you understand what you are changing. The most common cause of a heat pump underperforming after a year is that somebody adjusted something.
  • Notice changes. A new noise, a room that has stopped reaching temperature, hot water that runs short, or the system running far more than it used to are all worth reporting.
  • Check the pressure gauge occasionally, and report a falling reading rather than repeatedly topping it up. Repeated top-ups indicate a leak.

Common misconceptions, stated fairly

“Heat pumps are maintenance free.” They need less attention than a boiler and no gas safety check, but treating them as maintenance free tends to cost efficiency quietly and warranty cover eventually.

“Servicing is just a way of selling a contract.” It is recurring revenue, and Airwarm is open about that. It is also how performance is protected and how the manufacturer’s warranty stays valid. Judge it by what the visit actually checks and what you are given afterwards.

“Any heating engineer can service a heat pump.” Many competent engineers can carry out the water-side and electrical checks. Refrigerant work requires specific qualification, and manufacturer-specific controls and fault diagnosis benefit from familiarity with that equipment.

“If it is heating the house, it is fine.” A system can heat the house perfectly while running hotter than designed, and that is invisible without looking at the settings and the data.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a heat pump be serviced?

Annually is the normal recommendation and is commonly what manufacturers require for warranty purposes. Your handover documentation will state the requirement for your specific equipment.

Will you service a heat pump someone else installed?

Often yes, subject to make, model, condition, available documentation and whether it falls within Airwarm’s competence. Airwarm will say clearly if it does not.

Does missing a service void my warranty?

It can. Manufacturers commonly make documented annual servicing a warranty condition, and the terms vary. Check your specific warranty document, and keep the service records.
