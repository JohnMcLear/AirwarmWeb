Warmer homes

A heat pump works best when the whole system is designed to run at a lower temperature. Done properly, rooms hold a steady, even warmth rather than swinging between hot and cold.

Lower bills

A heat pump moves heat rather than burning fuel, so it delivers more heat than the electricity it uses. What that means for your bills depends on your home, your tariff and how the system is set up — which is exactly what a survey is for.

Clean energy

No gas, oil or LPG burnt in the home, no flue and no combustion products. As the electricity grid gets cleaner, so does the heating.

Suitability first

A good installation begins with the home, not the product

Airwarm checks the building, the radiators or underfloor heating, your hot-water requirements, the controls and the practical installation details before making a recommendation.

That order matters. A heat pump sized for a home that has not been assessed is the single most common reason people end up disappointed. If your property needs work first, we will say so and explain why. If a heat pump is not the right next step for you today, we will tell you that too.

What we look at

  • Building fabric and insulation
  • Radiator or underfloor emitter capacity
  • Hot-water demand and cylinder space
  • A sensible, quiet outdoor unit location
  • Controls and how you actually use the house
  • Electrical supply and practical pipe routes
The first step

The Home Energy Assessment

A quick first step to understand whether a heat pump is worth exploring for your property.

It is four short stages of straightforward questions about your home. No photographs. No measuring. Nothing to prepare. At the end you get a plain-English view of where your property stands and what a sensible next step would be.

The assessment is an indicative first step. It is not a technical heat-loss survey, and it does not produce a design or a price.
